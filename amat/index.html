Applied Materials, Inc. · Common stock · Santa Clara, California

AI chips need new materials, and Applied sells the tools.

Applied Materials makes the deposition, etch, inspection and packaging tools that chipmakers buy, and services the machines already in their fabs. Revenue rose 53% to a record $10.41B last quarter as DRAM rose to a third of chip-tool sales, and the January-quarter guide of $11.1B would be up 58%. The catch: China is still a quarter of sales under tightening US export rules, and the stock has nearly halved from its June peak.

Watch list

What moves the thesis, sorted by direction.

Tailwind

AI-driven mix

DRAM rose to 33% of chip-tool sales from 28% a year ago. Management sees advanced packaging up about 90% and process control up over 50% in calendar 2026, with strong growth again in 2027.

Tailwind

Record quarter and guide

Non-GAAP operating margin hit a record 35.3%, up 6.7 points in a year. The Q1 FY27 guide of $11.1B (a 14-week quarter) matches consensus and would be another record.

Watch

2027 outlook

Management expects chip-equipment spending to grow more than 30% in 2027 and Applied to beat the market, with leading-edge logic and DRAM fabs running full. Clean-room space is the main limit on shipments.

Watch

Capex build-out

Applied plans to double system output from Q4 2026 levels by 2028. Capex was $2.7B in FY26, so free cash flow rose only 9% to $6.2B while non-GAAP EPS rose 37%.

Risk

China and export rules

China was 25% of revenue, down from 29% a year ago, and is expected below 25% in 2027. The US Affiliates Rule, paused since Nov 2025, is due back after the trade truce ends Jan 10, 2027.

Risk

Cycle and valuation

At about 30 times trailing and 20 times forward earnings the stock prices in a long upcycle. It fell about 47% from its June peak to the Nov 5 low.

Calendar

Jan 10, 2027

US–China truce deadline

The paused export Affiliates Rule is set to return when the truce ends unless extended again.
