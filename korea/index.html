KOSPI ↔ KTB rotation

Korean retail money swings between stocks and bonds: when KOSPI runs, leveraged margin credit and broker deposits pile in and KTB demand fades; when stocks crack, deleveraging money parks in duration. This panel follows that switch with weekly flow proxies (KOFIA margin credit balance for leveraged equity money, and the 30Y KTB ETF\'s share of ETF turnover for bond demand) and combines them with equity and yield momentum into a Bond Rotation Signal, back-tested walk-forward. Same method as the China tab, so the two signals can be compared.

KOSPI vs 10Y KTB yield, with signal regimes

Shading shows the signal's regime each week. With the yield axis inverted, the two lines rise together when bonds and stocks rally together, and they diverge during a rotation. Arrows mark policy and flow events.

Flows: margin credit vs bond ETF demand

Bars: weekly change in KOFIA's margin credit balance (신용공여 잔고, KRW tn, left axis). Line: 30Y KTB ETF (KODEX 국고채30년액티브, 439870) turnover as a share of 439870 + KOSPI 200 ETF (KODEX 200, 069500) turnover (right axis). Rising bars with a falling line means money is moving into equities; falling bars with a rising line means it is moving back into bonds.

Bond Rotation Signal

Signal = average of four z-scores, each against its own trailing 52 weeks: −KOSPI 4-week return, −margin credit 4-week % change, +bond ETF turnover share vs its 26-week average, and −10Y KTB 4-week change. Above +0.5 reads as "bond rally" and below −0.5 as "bond sell-off". The optional pink line is the 26-week correlation of weekly KOSPI returns with weekly 10Y yield changes; it runs positive when the rotation is driving prices (stocks up, yields up).

US Treasury → KTB transmission

Korean bonds open each day after New York has closed, so the overnight US Treasury move is the first thing KTBs price. This panel measures how much of it carries over, and the back-test below checks whether the rotation signal works better once that global part is stripped out.

    Funding and credit

    Korea publishes no public bond lending fee (KTB borrowing is arranged bilaterally through the Korea Securities Depository and brokers), so this section tracks the funding and credit rates that drive KTB demand: KOFIA's CD, CP and MSB 91-day quotes, the KDB 1-year, and 3-year KEPCO and corporate credit spreads.

    Bonds by code and ISIN

    Every Korea Treasury Bond seen trading over the counter in the last year, plus Monetary Stabilisation Bonds, KDB, IBK and KEXIM bonds, and AAA bank debentures traded in the latest session, one row per bond with its issue code and ISIN. Curve yield is the issuer's curve read at the bond's remaining term; traded yield is KOFIA's volume-weighted average OTC yield for the session, and "vs curve" is the difference in basis points.

    KTB curve and tenor history: KOFIA bond information service (kofiabond.or.kr) final-quote yields (최종호가수익률) at 15:30 KST, the market's official close, for the 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30 and 50-year KTB benchmarks; the curve is joined smoothly (monotone cubic) between them and runs to the 91-day MSB quote below 1 year. MSB curve: KOFIA 91-day, 1-year and 2-year MSB quotes. KDB, IBK, KEXIM and AAA bank debenture curves: fitted by the dashboard (KTB curve plus a Nelson-Siegel spread fitted to the issuer's KOFIA OTC trades over the last three sessions); KOFIA publishes a 1-year KDB quote for comparison. Bonds, ISINs and traded yields: KOFIA daily OTC trade summary by bond (일자별 거래현황); coupons from the bond name, maturities from KOFIA's remaining term. KTB futures: KOFIA delivery-basket yields (국채선물수익률) with KRX's settlement formula; fair value, not a traded price. Funding and credit: KOFIA CD, CP, MSB, KDB, KEPCO and corporate final quotes. KOSPI ↔ KTB rotation: Naver Finance (KOSPI, KODEX ETFs 439870 and 069500), KOFIA freesis (margin credit balance, investor deposits), KOFIA 10Y KTB; signal and back-test computed by the dashboard.
