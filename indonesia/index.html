Foreign flows · Indonesian equities, rupiah and INDOGBs · weekly

JCI ↔ INDOGB flows

Indonesia is a foreign-money market: overseas investors hold a large slice of both the stock market's free float and tradable government bonds, and the rupiah is the channel they move through. So unlike Korea's retail rotation, Indonesian stocks, the rupiah and bonds have tended to sell off and recover together. This panel follows that with weekly proxies (JCI, USD/IDR, the 10Y INDOGB trend and the INDOGB − UST spread) combined into a Bond Flow Signal, back-tested walk-forward with the same rules as the China and Korea tabs. The Korea-style rotation is tested alongside it as a variant.

JCI vs 10Y INDOGB yield, with signal regimes

Shading shows the signal's regime each week. With the yield axis inverted, the two lines rise together when bonds and stocks rally together, and they diverge during a rotation. Arrows mark policy and flow events.

Flows: rupiah vs the INDOGB − UST spread

Bars: weekly change in USD/IDR (red = rupiah weaker, the usual sign of foreign selling; green = stronger). Line: the 10Y INDOGB yield minus the 10Y US Treasury, in bp (right axis), the pick-up a dollar investor earns before currency risk. A falling rupiah with a widening spread is foreign money leaving; a firming rupiah with a narrowing spread is money coming back.

Bond Flow Signal

Signal = average of four z-scores, each against its own trailing 52 weeks: +JCI 4-week return, −USD/IDR 4-week % change, −10Y INDOGB 4-week change, and +INDOGB − UST spread versus its 26-week average (wide = cheap). Above +0.5 reads as "bond rally" and below −0.5 as "bond sell-off". The optional pink line is the 26-week correlation of weekly JCI returns with weekly 10Y yield changes; it runs negative when stocks and bonds rally together (stocks up, yields down), as foreign flows imply.

    Bonds by code and ISIN

    Every government security on PHEI's register (conventional SUN: FR, ORI; sukuk SBSN: PBS, IFR), plus the retail SR sukuk PHEI prices, one row per series with its code and ISIN, outstanding amount, coupon and maturity. Curve yield is IGSYC at the bond's remaining term (for PBS and retail series, the fitted sukuk or retail curve); PHEI fair is PHEI's published fair yield where PHEI releases one, and "vs curve" is the difference in basis points.

    IGSYC curve and tenor history: PHEI / IBPA (PT Penilai Harga Efek Indonesia, phei.co.id) Indonesia Government Securities Yield Curve, published each business day from 0.1 to 40 years; the dashboard stores each day because PHEI shows only the latest two, and before its first stored day the 10Y and 15Y run on CNBC's benchmark-bond yields, spliced to IGSYC with an offset re-estimated every run. Benchmark, sukuk and retail fair yields and prices: PHEI fair prices and yield (Harga Pasar Wajar). PBS sukuk and retail ORI / SR curves: IGSYC plus a Nelson-Siegel spread fitted by the dashboard to PHEI's fair yields. Corporate AAA–BBB curves: PHEI corporate bond yield by tenor and credit spread matrix. Bonds, ISINs, outstanding, coupons and maturities: PHEI securities information (Informasi Efek); SR maturities are estimated from PHEI's remaining term. BI-Rate and JISDOR: Bank Indonesia (bi.go.id); USD/IDR before the first stored JISDOR day from Yahoo Finance. JCI ↔ INDOGB flows: Yahoo Finance (^JKSE, IDR=X), US Treasury daily par yields; signal and back-tests computed by the dashboard. Indicative, not an offer to trade.
