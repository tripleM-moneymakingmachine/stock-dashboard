Rockwell Automation, Inc. · Common stock · Milwaukee, Wisconsin

Factory automation, pulled along by chip fabs and data centers.

Rockwell sells the controllers, drives, sensors and software that run factories, warehouses and process plants. September-quarter sales rose only 1% to $2.33B and missed estimates, because Rockwell handed tariff refunds back to customers; without that and a divestiture, core sales grew 7%. The first FY27 outlook calls for 6–9% organic growth and adjusted EPS of $14.50–15.50. The stock fell 14% on the report and now trades at about 26 times next year's earnings.

Watch list

What moves the thesis, sorted by direction.

Tailwind

Chips and data centers

Semiconductor and data center, reported as one vertical from FY27, are about 10% of sales and expected to grow mid-teens. Hyperscale operator QTS standardized on Rockwell in Q4.

Tailwind

Margins expanding

Enterprise operating margin was above 22% for three straight quarters, hitting the long-term target early; FY27 guides about 23%. New targets come at the Nov 18 investor day.

Watch

Soft first quarter

Q1 FY27 adjusted EPS is guided near $3.00, with organic sales up mid-single digits and about $50M of lost Sensia sales in each of Q1 and Q2.

Watch

Recurring revenue

Organic annual recurring revenue grew 5% in FY26, slower than before; management expects it to pick up to 7% in FY27.

Risk

Costs and project timing

Memory and circuit-board inflation has not eased, mining sales fell about 10% on project delays, and capex rises to about 4% of sales for a new US plant.

Risk

Valuation

The FY27 EPS midpoint is only about 1% above consensus. Even after the 14% drop, the stock trades at about 26 times next year's earnings.
