Fund flows · China equities vs CGBs · weekly

Bond ↔ equity rotation

China stocks and bonds tend to move inversely: when equities slump, money hides in CGBs, and when policy revives stocks, funds sell duration to buy equities. This panel follows that switch with weekly flow proxies (margin financing debt for leveraged equity money, and the 30Y CGB ETF's share of ETF turnover for bond demand). It combines them with equity and yield momentum into a Bond Rotation Signal, back-tested walk-forward over the full history.

CSI 300 vs 10Y CGB yield, with signal regimes

Shading shows the signal's regime each week. With the yield axis inverted, the two lines rise together when bonds and stocks rally together, and they diverge during a rotation. Arrows mark policy and flow events.

Flows: margin debt vs bond ETF demand

Bars: weekly change in the Shanghai + Shenzhen margin financing balance (RMB bn, left axis). Line: 30Y CGB ETF (511090) turnover as a share of 511090 + CSI 300 ETF (510300) turnover (right axis). Rising bars with a falling line means money is moving into equities. Falling bars with a rising line means it is moving back into bonds.

Bond Rotation Signal

Signal = average of four z-scores, each measured against its own trailing 52 weeks: −CSI 300 4-week return, −margin balance 4-week % change, +bond ETF turnover share vs its 26-week average, and −10Y 4-week change. Above +0.5 reads as "bond rally" and below −0.5 as "bond sell-off". The optional pink line is the 26-week correlation of weekly CSI 300 returns with weekly 10Y yield changes. It runs positive when the rotation is driving prices (stocks up, yields up).

    Monthly volume by bond type

    By bond code / ISIN: CFETS and ChinaBond (CCDC) publish lending fees only by tenor (L001 overnight … L1Y), plus monthly volumes by bond type. Neither releases the fee for an individual bond code or ISIN — those are bilateral quotes visible only to members on the CFETS terminal — so the bond table below stays on yields.
